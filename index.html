PFAS Exposure Index

A potency-weighted cumulative-dose score. Each compound is divided by its health-based reference (a Hazard Quotient), the quotients are summed into a Hazard Index, and that is multiplied by years of residence — then summed across every address. Higher = stronger, longer exposure.

Cutoffs are placeholders — recalibrate on your docket's HI-year distribution. One HI-year = a full year at exactly the health-based reference for a single compound.

▲ Below the PFOA/PFOS floor. Neither PFOA nor PFOS reached its 4 ppt MCL at any residence. These are the strongest-causation compounds — treat this as a weak-exposure file and consider screening before tiering.
▲ Low-confidence data. At least one residence uses a peak or single-sample reading rather than a time-weighted average. Verify against annual-average supplier data before relying on the tier.
Exposure only. This scores contamination and duration — not injury. Combine it with a diagnosis and causation axis before valuing a case, so a deep-exposure claimant with a non-linked condition doesn't outrank a moderate-exposure claimant with kidney or testicular cancer.
Regulatory status. The PFOA and PFOS MCLs (4 ppt) are upheld. The 10 ppt individual limits and the Hazard Index are under a proposed EPA rescission (comment period closed July 20, 2026; final action expected in 2026). The method rests on health-based reference values and standard mixtures toxicology, which survive the rulemaking — but in public content, don't cite 10 ppt as a current federal limit without noting the pending rescission.
